# Sweetwater Product Development Funding Plan

## Objective

Use Sweetwater as a product-development, market-validation, and possible exclusive launch partner for System Q / GIG.

Do not frame Sweetwater as the primary manufacturer or final acquirer at first.

The strongest Sweetwater thesis is:

> System Q may be a new recording category that Sweetwater can validate, shape, and potentially launch exclusively before the broader market understands it.

## Why Sweetwater may care

Sweetwater is not just a store.

Its useful assets are:

- direct access to recording customers
- trusted sales engineers who know what musicians ask for and misunderstand
- Sweetwater Studios as a credible evaluation and demo environment
- strong education/content capability
- ability to validate whether a new product category is sellable
- potential to structure an exclusive retail or launch relationship

System Q needs exactly that kind of market-facing pressure test.

## What Sweetwater should not be asked to do first

Do not open with:

- "Fund the entire company."
- "Acquire System Q."
- "Build all the hardware."
- "Become the manufacturer."
- "Evaluate the full AIP / Guardian strategy."

Those asks are too broad and put the wrong burden on Sweetwater.

The first ask should be staged and commercially rational.

## Proposed deal structure

### Stage 1: Paid category evaluation

Sweetwater funds a short evaluation period.

Participants:

- Sweetwater Studios
- senior pro-audio sales engineers
- recording/category managers
- outside technical validator if needed

Materials reviewed:

- System Q one-page overview
- product architecture brief
- current software screenshots
- Tape Cylinder / analog card summary
- proposed first proof-of-concept

Output:

- written category assessment
- customer-language feedback
- objections and confusion points
- recommended minimum product entry point

Goal:

Decide whether Sweetwater sees a customer-understandable category.

### Stage 2: Customer discovery sprint

Sweetwater helps test the product story with a controlled group of recording customers.

Possible groups:

- project studio owners
- church / worship production teams
- producer-engineers
- live-sound engineers who also record
- serious home-studio musicians
- educational studio buyers

Questions:

- Do customers understand the workflow?
- Which product surface is easiest to buy first: Rig, Cube, Rack, Console, or software?
- Do historic input/output cards create desire or confusion?
- Does Tape Cylinder read as meaningful or too speculative?
- What price band feels plausible?
- What existing purchases would this replace or complement?

Output:

- customer discovery summary
- ranked use cases
- strongest buyer segment
- minimum sellable configuration

### Stage 3: Prototype funding option

If Stage 1 and Stage 2 show real demand, Sweetwater funds or co-funds a focused proof-of-concept.

The prototype should not be the whole ecosystem.

Recommended first prototype:

- System Q software control surface
- one simple Cube-style input path or rack mock path
- one analog card proof path
- one Tape Cylinder or magnetic-buffer bench demonstration if technically validated
- a sellable demo workflow: plug in, track, shape, print, play back

Partner roles:

- Sweetwater: customer validation, demo environment, possible funding, launch path
- Paul Wolff / hardware validator: analog topology review
- Bob Clearmountain / musical validator: mix and workflow credibility
- Apogee or another manufacturer: possible hardware/product partner if validation lands

### Stage 4: Exclusive launch option

Sweetwater receives an option for:

- first retail launch
- limited exclusive bundle
- Sweetwater Studios launch/demo content
- early customer education
- product feedback loop from sales engineers

This gives Sweetwater upside without forcing them to become the manufacturer.

### Stage 5: Manufacturer pairing

If validation is strong, bring in a manufacturer.

Candidate manufacturer types:

- Apogee for premium recording trust and conversion
- Wolff / Fix Audio-style analog partner for card/rack path
- UA / Focusrite / SSL / Audient / PreSonus type partner for broader productization
- manufacturing partner for enclosure, supply chain, certification, support

Sweetwater's role becomes launch channel and market validator.

## Minimum package before approaching Sweetwater

Do not approach Sweetwater with only the broad vision.

Prepare:

1. one-page System Q overview
2. product architecture brief
3. current software screenshot
4. short Sweetwater-specific memo
5. proposed Stage 1 evaluation scope
6. proposed budget range
7. clear statement that Sweetwater is not being asked to manufacture the full system

## Proposed Stage 1 budget framing

Do not start with a giant number.

A reasonable first ask could be framed as:

> I am looking for a paid category-development review, not a full investment commitment. The goal is to determine whether Sweetwater sees a customer-understandable product category here and what the first minimum sellable configuration should be.

Possible budget bands:

- $5k-$10k: basic paid review / expert feedback
- $15k-$25k: structured category evaluation with Sweetwater Studios and sales-engineer input
- $50k-$100k: customer discovery plus early prototype planning
- $250k+: prototype sponsorship or exclusive-development option

The first outreach should not specify the high number unless Sweetwater asks about development funding.

> Is this a customer-understandable recording category, and what would the first sellable version need to be?

1. Sweetwater Studios / pro-audio team reviews the concept,
2. sales engineers and selected customers pressure-test the story,
3. we identify the smallest sellable configuration,

Thanks,

Craig

## Sequence recommendation

Best order:

1. Paul Wolff: analog architecture feasibility
2. Bob Clearmountain: musical / mix credibility and Apogee access
3. Sweetwater: customer validation and staged development funding
4. Apogee or manufacturer: productization / acquisition / partnership

If Sweetwater is approached before Paul or Bob, keep the ask limited to market validation and avoid deep technical claims.

## Decision gates

Do not advance to prototype funding unless these are answered:

- Is the buyer segment clear?
- Is the first sellable configuration clear?
- Is the analog card story credible after technical review?
- Is Tape Cylinder essential to the first product, or should it remain a later flagship feature?
- Can Sweetwater explain the product in one sentence to customers?
- Is there a manufacturer path that does not depend on Sweetwater becoming a hardware R&D company?

## Working conclusion

Sweetwater is not the first-choice builder.

Sweetwater may be a strong development-funding and launch partner if the ask is staged:

> Help validate and shape the first sellable System Q product, then take an exclusive or first-launch position if the category proves real.
